# Dossier: <domain>

_Last updated: YYYY-MM-DD_

This is the durable asset. Individual board runs are disposable; this file is not. Update it
rather than rewriting it, and keep it honest even where that is uncomfortable. The board is only
as good as this document. Vague input produces the generic AI advice you were trying to escape.

Delete any section that genuinely does not apply. Do not leave a section in place with a
placeholder answer, because a placeholder reads to the board as a fact.

---

## The situation
What is actually being decided, and why now. What changed to make this live. What is already
settled and not up for debate.

## The people
A profile of every person materially affected. For each: age, what their day currently looks
like, what they need, what they are good at, what constrains them.

Name people. "Our child" produces checklist analysis; a named person with a described Tuesday
produces something you can act on.

State hard constraints as facts, in their own sentence. A constraint buried mid-paragraph gets
optimized around rather than respected.

## The money
How income is earned, what is on the horizon, and the actual numbers. Fixed costs, variable
costs, savings, debts, runway. What is committed and what is discretionary.

Real figures or the board will invent plausible ones and reason from those.

## The obligations
What has to be managed, maintained, or shown up for regardless of the decision. Then the question
most people never ask: does it have to be managed *from where it is*? Separate the obligation
from the assumed location of the obligation.

## The options
Each candidate under consideration, with what is known and what is still unknown about it.
Include the option of doing nothing, stated as seriously as the others.

## What we will not trade away
The hard floor. What makes an option disqualified regardless of how well it scores elsewhere.

Be strict here. Everything not on this list is negotiable, and the board will treat it that way.

## What good looks like
What daily life should feel like if this goes right. Not metrics. The texture of it.

## Open questions
What you do not know and have not been able to find out. Naming a gap is more useful than filling
it with a guess: the board can reason around a known unknown, but not around a confident error.
